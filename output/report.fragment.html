Normalised Bollywood Earnings

Hindi film box office in rupees, normalised for ticket-price inflation. Each film's domestic nett collection is converted to the number of tickets it sold, using the average Hindi ticket price of its release year, and those tickets are re-priced at the 2025 ticket. The result is what each film would have earned at today's prices, so releases from different decades can be compared directly.

Method

Worked example: Sholay (1975) and 3 Idiots (2009)

Options that we can normalise

  • Tax basis. All figures are domestic India nett, after entertainment tax or GST. Gross is not used: before 2017 it could be twice nett in some states.
  • Ticket price. Footfalls = nett ÷ average Hindi nett ticket price of the release year. Normalised box office = footfalls × 2025 ticket price. The two rank identically.
  • Population. Footfalls per 1,000 people in the release year.
  • Industry size. Nett ÷ median nett of the other films in that year's top 10. Blank where a year has fewer than five tracked films.
  • CPI. Nett × CPI(2025) ÷ CPI(year). Shown for comparison; it understates pre-2005 films because ticket prices outran inflation.
  • Re-releases. Excluded where the source separates them.
  • Confidence. Tier A: footfalls published by a trade source (±10%). Tier B: derived from tracked nett, 1994 onward (±15%). Tier C: derived from reconstructed pre-1994 nett (±25% from 1975, ±40% earlier).

Limitations

  • India has no official admissions tracking; all footfalls are trade estimates.
  • Pre-1994 nett figures are retrospective reconstructions from historical Box Office India tables.
  • The 1957, 1960 and 1975 price anchors are set from the consensus footfalls of Mother India, Mughal-e-Azam and Sholay, so those three positions follow from the anchors rather than from independent data.
  • Published 1980s footfall claims (Coolie, Maine Pyar Kiya) imply a flat ₹2 ticket for the decade, inconsistent with the ₹11 recorded for 1994. They appear only in the consensus column.
  • Box Office India's structured data ends in mid-2024; 2024–26 footfalls come from its articles and from Bollywood Hungama.
  • Not adjusted for: screen count and seat capacity, piracy, streaming windows, release competition.
